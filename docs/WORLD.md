# Divine Mismanagement — world, story and tone

The setting, the cast, the shape of the story, and the rules for how the game
talks. Drafted 2026-11-14 for the author's approval. Everything named here is
invented for this game; section 9 lists what must be reconciled with the sister
TTRPG *Acts of God*.

---

## 1. Theme

**Nobody is in charge, least of all the one in charge.**

Three ideas, in the order the player meets them:

1. *Message is not meaning.* What you say and what is heard are different
   objects. (The telephone game.)
2. *Writing it down makes it worse.* A rule outlives its reason. (Scripture.)
3. *The institution does not need you.* Past a certain size, the church runs on
   committees and precedent and treats its god as a stakeholder. (The late
   game.)

The game is fond of everybody in it. The followers are fools the way everyone
is a fool at work on a Monday. The comedy is in procedures, pride and
paperwork.

## 2. The Overcrowded Heavens

- **Belief is the only currency.** Gods are sustained by being believed in. A
  god nobody thinks about goes translucent, then gets archived.
- **There are far too many gods.** Every hill, craft, inconvenience and mood
  has one. Domains have been subdivided for so long that new gods are issued
  whatever is left.
- **Heaven is run as an office.** Nobody designed it that way. It accreted.

| Place | What it is |
|---|---|
| **The High Pantheon** | Seven seats at the top. One has been empty for an age; the vacancy is why anyone tries |
| **The Middle Heavens** | Established gods with temples, staff and opinions about parking |
| **The Lower Annex** | Where minor gods share desks. Yours has a wobble |
| **The Registry** | Records every god, domain and believer. Conducts Reviews. Has never lost a form and has never found one quickly |
| **The Archive** | Where forgotten gods are filed. Quiet. Nobody visits |

- **The rules gods live by** (these are also the game's rules):
  a god may not speak to mortals in plain terms, only through a chosen Prophet
  ("the Indirection Ordinance": passed after an incident nobody will describe);
  miracles are permitted but logged; poaching believers is legal and
  considered good sport.

## 3. The mortal world

One small, damp, cheerful country of villages, each worked by several gods at
once. Nobody mortal finds this strange; you keep a few gods the way you keep a
few hens.

**The village (v0.1): Little Fretting.** Thirty-six souls, a mill, an alehouse,
a pond with eels in it, and a standing stone nobody remembers the reason for
(a previous god; archived). Twelve villagers are yours. Twelve belong to your
rival. Twelve are **the Unbothered**, who believe in lunch.

The four households of your flock are generated per run, each with a trade:
millers, alewives, shepherds, carpenters, eel-catchers, beekeepers, a
self-appointed clerk, and so on. Names come from a table built to sound like
one place (Odo, Brenna, Wilf, Cuthwin, Hesper, Maud, Tibb, Aldous, Pernel).

## 4. The cast

### You
A newly registered minor god. The player supplies the name. From the slice
onward the player picks a domain; each is a lean, not a class:

| Domain (draft) | Lean | Claimed difference (to be measured) |
|---|---|---|
| God of Doors That Stick | Thresholds, patience | Silence builds Mystery faster; miracles cost more |
| Goddess of Lukewarm Soup | Comfort, adequacy | Answered prayers give more; Oddities give less |
| God of Lost Left Shoes | Small losses, searching | Garbled verses (Oddities) give more; plain verses give less |

More domains are cheap to add once three are measured. All must be oddly
specific, harmless and a little sad.

### Pim, your caseworker
A seraph, third class, from the Registry's Office of Minor Deity Affairs. Small,
round, winged, wears spectacles, carries a clipboard larger than itself. Pim is
the tutorial, the turn digest and the year-end memo. Dry, overworked, privately
on your side. Pim never lies to you and never flatters you.

> "Good morning. You have twelve believers. The Registry considers that a
> rounding error, but I have seen worse. I have not seen much worse."

### Vellamine the Radiant, Goddess of Being Noticed (the v0.1 rival)
Arrived the same week you did and already has a logo. Her missionaries are
charming and her miracles are mostly lighting. She is not evil; she is
*popular*, which is harder to forgive. She plays by the same rules, and her
Scripture comes out of her own twelve idiots just as mangled as yours. Reading
it is one of the game's rewards.

Escalation: mockery of your Scripture, then a missionary, then a festival, then
a counter-miracle, then an open offer to absorb you "as a valued sub-brand".

### Later rivals (not v0.1; one line each so the bench exists)
- **Hurm, God of Fences** (and by extension, Neighbours). Litigious.
- **Ysolde, Goddess of Unsolicited Advice.** Converts by correcting.
- **Old Brannoch, God of Slightly Better Bread.** Wins on merit, which is
  infuriating.

### The followers
Twelve named people with a trade, one or two traits, a devotion level, family
and grudges, and one personal want that drives their prayers (a husband, a
bigger pig, to be taken seriously, for Wilf to return the ladder). They are
generated, so the table of wants and trades carries the charm. Each trait has a
visible prop on the sprite (`DESIGN.md` section 5) so the player learns to read
the village at a glance.

## 5. The story

The game is systemic, so the story is a frame with fixed beats and a middle the
player's Scripture fills in.

| Era | Turns | What changes | Fixed beats |
|---|---|---|---|
| **I. The Village** (v0.1) | 1-30 | Twelve idiots, first verses, one rival | Pim's induction. First verse carved. Vellamine's missionary arrives. The first Oddity becomes a festival. First Review |
| **II. The Town** | 31-60 | Priests, tithes, a second generation that inherits traits and misremembers the verses | The first funeral and the first wrong saint. A verse you wrote is quoted against you. The first schism threat |
| **III. The Temple** | 61-90 | The church has committees. Councils reinterpret Scripture without asking | A council votes on what you meant. You are invited to your own festival as a guest. Final Review for the vacant seat |

**The turn the story takes.** In Era I the problem is that they do not
understand you. In Era III the problem is that they no longer need to. The
winning player ends up running a religion built almost entirely out of their
own mistakes, and has grown fond of it.

**Endings.**

| Ending | How | Tone |
|---|---|---|
| **Ascended** | Pass the final Review | You take the seventh seat. Pim is reassigned to a new minor god and sends you a form to sign |
| **Forgotten** | Faith at zero | Filed in the Archive between a god of a river that moved and a god of a word nobody uses |
| **Absorbed** | The rival takes the majority | Saint of something small in her faith. Your feast day is a Tuesday |
| **Schism** | Heresy splits the flock | Two lesser gods, one desk, alternating days |

Every ending recites the run's Scripture back, names the verse that did the
most damage, and names the follower responsible.

## 6. Tone: the satire and sass dial

*2026-11-14: the author asked that it not look like a children's game.
`FRAME-management.md` section 2 proposes Sass 4, Bite 3, Darkness 2 and
withdraws the "twelve-year-old" line in hard rule 7. The table below is the
earlier setting until that is approved.*

Settings on a five-point scale. These are the brief's "level of satire and
sass" written down so every table of phrases can be checked against them.

| Dial | Setting | What that means in practice |
|---|---|---|
| **Warmth** | 4 / 5 | The village is likeable. Insults are affectionate. Nobody is humiliated for being poor, old, ill or slow |
| **Absurdity** | 4 / 5 | Logic is followed carefully to a silly place. The eel is treated with full seriousness by everyone |
| **Sass** | 3 / 5 | Pim and the rival get the dry lines. The narration does not wink at the player or mention being a game |
| **Bite** | 2 / 5 | Targets are bureaucracy, zealotry, ego and doctrine creep. It stings the way a good workplace joke stings |
| **Darkness** | 1 / 5 | Nobody suffers on screen. Smiting is slapstick (singed eyebrows). Death is "gone to file his paperwork" |

### Hard rules

1. **Invented gods only.** No real deity, prophet, scripture, sect, holiday or
   holy place, by name or by obvious likeness.
2. **Never quote or paraphrase a real verse, commandment or prayer.** A
   Revelation template that resembles one is rewritten (no day-of-rest rule
   tied to a numbered day, no list of ten of anything).
3. **Generic vocabulary only:** prophet, priest, saint, scripture, temple,
   tithe, heresy, blessing. No term that belongs to one living tradition.
   Mock-archaic phrasing is seasoning ("lest", "henceforth"), used rarely.
4. **Punch at behaviour and at the player.** The follower is wrong in a way the
   player recognises in themselves or their workplace.
5. **The joke is in the system first.** A phrase table entry must be funny in
   more than one context or it is a one-off and belongs in an event.
6. **Short.** A verse fits on a phone in two lines (140 characters). A Pim line
   is one or two sentences.
7. **No cruelty, no crudeness, no real-world politics.** A twelve-year-old and
   a parish treasurer should both be able to play it.

### Voice samples

**A Revelation, shown the way the game shows it.**

> **You said:** The faithful should wash their hands before meals.
> **Odo the Miller** (Lazy) passed on: *The faithful should wash before meals.*
> **Brenna the Alewife** (Drunk) passed on: *The faithful should wash before eels.*
> **Cuthwin the Shepherd** (Zealot) passed on: *ALL must wash before eels, or be put in the pond.*
> **Carved:** the Verse of the Eel.
> *An eel is now kept in a tub by the temple door so that people may wash in front of it. Hygiene is unchanged. Mystery +6. The eel-catchers are thrilled.*

> **You said:** The faithful should welcome strangers kindly.
> **Hesper the Beekeeper** (Poet): *Let the stranger be as a warm loaf among you.*
> **Aldous the Clerk** (Literalist): *Strangers are to be treated as warm loaves.*
> **Tibb the Carpenter** (Lazy): *Strangers are loaves.*
> **Carved:** the Verse of the Loaf.
> *Vellamine's missionary was buttered on arrival and has asked to be reassigned.*

> **You said:** The faithful should not borrow tools without asking.
> **Brenna** (Drunk): *The faithful should not borrow stools without asking.*
> **Wilf the Carpenter** (Ambitious): *...and all stools are to be bought new, from Wilf.*
> **Carved:** the Verse of the Stool.
> *Wilf's devotion has risen sharply. So has Wilf.*

> **You said:** The faithful may rest after the harvest.
> **Cuthwin** (Zealot): *The faithful must rest after the harvest.*
> **Aldous** (Literalist): *The faithful must rest immediately after the harvest, where they stand.*
> *The village spent a night asleep in the barley. Cheer +2. Bread -1. Three colds.*

**Pim.**
- Turn digest: "Faith is up four. I would like to say it was something you did."
- On silence: "You said nothing this season. They found it very moving."
- On a third miracle in a row: "They have stopped saying thank you and started
  saying *and another thing*."
- Year-end memo: "The Registry notes your religion now forbids stools on
  credit. The Registry does not ask why. The Registry is simply noting it."

**Vellamine.**
- "I read your new verse. Brave! I could never be that unclear."

**A prayer.**
- "O god of whatever it is: Maud here. It is about the pig again."

**Empty and locked states keep the voice but always name the lever.**
- "No Power left. Power comes from Faith each season; a silent season costs
  nothing."

## 7. Content this implies (the real cost of the game)

*2026-11-14: the no-repeat rule raises several of these targets. The current
table is in `FRAME-management.md` section 3.*

The brief is right that the phrase library is the investment. Targets for v0.1:

| Table | v0.1 target | Why that many |
|---|---|---|
| Revelation templates | 5, each with 4-6 swappable blocks per slot | Brief minimum |
| Drunk sound-alike pairs | 80 | The trait most likely to repeat |
| Poet metaphors | 40, tagged by domain | |
| Ambitious riders | 3 per trade x 12 trades | The rider must name the speaker's trade |
| Zealot penalties | 20 | "or be put in the pond" |
| Literalist expansions | 20, by slot | |
| Oddity outcomes | 2 per object | The line after "Carved:" |
| Pim lines | 60, keyed to state | Heard every turn |
| Wants and prayers | 30 wants, 2 prayers each | |
| Events | 12 | Brief build order item 6 |

Draws are without replacement within a run (state kept in the save), and a
tool counts repeats across 1,000 seeded chains so repetition is measured, not
guessed.

## 8. Names and words, for consistency

| Use | Not |
|---|---|
| Revelation (what you send) | Message, command |
| Verse (one carved result); Scripture (all of them); Tablets (the seven active); Apocrypha (retired) | Rule, law, commandment |
| Oddity (a garbled verse's ritual) | Bug, mistake |
| The flock, the faithful | Units, population |
| Review, Registry, memo | Level, boss, score |
| Stay Silent | Skip, pass |

## 9. To reconcile with *Acts of God*

This repository has not seen the TTRPG's text. Everything below was invented
here and should be checked against it by the author before it hardens:

- The structure of Heaven (High Pantheon of seven, Middle Heavens, Lower Annex,
  Registry, Archive).
- The Indirection Ordinance as the reason gods cannot speak plainly.
- "Archived" as what happens to forgotten gods.
- Pim, Vellamine, Hurm, Ysolde, Brannoch; the village of Little Fretting.
- Belief as the single currency, with no separate afterlife economy.
