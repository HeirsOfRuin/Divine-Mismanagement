# Frame: a story worth winning (playtest 3)

2026-11-15. **Status: calibration.** The backstory, the cast, five story scenes
with choices that come back later, rank scenes, the new Book and the ending are
built into Story Chapter 1 for the author's verdict. The rest of the spine is
written down here and not built.

## The author's words

> "It definitely paced it better, yes. Still fealt overall generic and the
> story was pointless, no real backstory, narrative, or feeling of investment
> or accomplishment."

On the Registry's goals: "Mostly noise". On the step-by-step playback: "Nah it
wasn't great, more confusing or noise than anything". On the Book: "No, the
book was the exact thing I had in my journal".

## Classification

A **design disagreement** with the build, and a fair one. The workflow's
calibration and vertical-slice gates (author verdict on voice, and "wants to
play the next one") were passed over in favour of systems. This frame goes back
to them.

## What was wrong

1. **You had no past and nothing personal to win.** "Promoted" meant a bigger
   desk. Nothing was taken from you, so nothing could be won back.
2. **Nobody to care about.** Twelve random names with traits are instruments,
   not people. Isolde ran the player's religion and was still only a name.
3. **The rival had no relationship to you.** A name, a sun, some lines.
4. **Nothing happened that would not have happened anyway.** Random problems,
   random lines. No choice came back later.
5. **No moment of accomplishment.** A rank was a line of news; winning was a
   paragraph.
6. **The Book repeated what the player had already read.**
7. **Noise:** the Registry's goals (checkboxes with no story) and the
   strike-through playback.

## The backstory

**You were the God of Thresholds**: doors, gates, stiles, the moment before a
decision. A respectable middle-sized god with temples in two market towns.

**Then the Registry re-filed everything**, and your form went through three
clerks. The first wrote "Thresholds That Stick". The second tidied it to
"Doors That Stick". The third, who liked things short, struck out your temples
as a duplicate entry. **What happens to your words in Little Fretting already
happened to you in Heaven.** The game's joke is now your history.

**Your old desk went to Vellamine**, Goddess of Being Noticed, who had applied
for something else and did not complain. **Pim**, seraph third class, was the
clerk who copied your form. Pim is very sorry, and is your caseworker.

**The Review is your appeal.** Pass it and your file can be corrected: you
could be the God of Thresholds again. Whether you want that by then is the last
choice of the game.

## The cast (Story mode)

in the story. The other eight are generated as before.

## The Book, as a payoff

The Book is no longer the Tablets copied out. It is **the village's account of
what you did**, written by Hesper years later and copied by a second scribe:
how it began (season 1, as the village remembers it), the words you gave (the
Tablets, retitled), the wonders (your miracles as told), the saints, the other
god, the committee. Each passage is told from what actually happened in your
game, then bent by the scribe's trait. **You know what really happened; the
Book is what they believe happened.** That gap is the joke and the reward.

## Noise removed

- **The Registry's goals are gone.** The story's goal (the appeal) replaces
  them.
- **The playback** is now three lines: what you said, what was carved, what
  happened. "How it got that way" is one tap away, in plain sentences, with
  no strike-through.

## What will be measured

1. Careful play in Chapter 1 without the goals, and with the scenes' effects,
   against the 30-40% target.
2. That every scene fires once, in order, in every story game, and that every
   choice's callback is reached (a test).
3. Nothing about whether it is now worth playing. That is the author's verdict.
