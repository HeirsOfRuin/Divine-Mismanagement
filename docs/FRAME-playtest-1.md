# Frame: playtest 1 — easier to use, more to do, introduced gradually, and a map

2026-11-14. The first playtest response from the author.

## The author's words

> "Meh, not very easy to use UI right now. And not overly fun. It needs more
> complexity that is introduced properly and with just enough visible so as not
> to overwhelm. And a region map would help when there are multiple villages in
> a region and multiple regions"

## Classification (game-building playtest protocol)

Not a bug. A **missing explanation**, a **missing control**, and a **design
disagreement**, in that order.

## What was wrong, looking at the build

1. **Everything at once.** Season one showed eight acts, four rare acts (with a
   paragraph each on why they were locked), the Registry line, the memo, the
   rival's line and a problem. No order of learning.
2. **No direction.** Nothing said what a good next move was. Every season was
   the same menu.
3. **The best joke was a list.** The telephone result arrived as paragraphs in
   a report, after the fact.
4. **Staffing by dropdown.** Twelve names in a select, with no sight of what a
   person would do in the post until after choosing them.
5. **Regions were coloured bars.** No sense of place, so where to send the
   missionary was not a decision anyone could feel.

## The design rule

**Show what can be done now, explain it the first time, and give the player a
reason to do it this season.**

## What changes

| Part | Change |
|---|---|
| **Guided start** (on by default) | Things open one at a time over the first two years: season 1 the Prophet, Preacher and Revelations; 2 the Missionary and prayers; 3 the Collector, Builder and Works; 4 the Witness and miracles; 6 dreams and festivals; 8 the other gods (smiting, answering their neglected, disputations). Each opening comes with a short note from Pim. What is not open yet is not shown. Rare acts appear only once their rank is reached. Custom matches can turn it off |
| **The Registry asks** | One or two short goals at a time ("Carve a Tablet that does some good"; "Fill three offices"; "Have more souls than Vellamine"), each with a small reward of Power, Faith or Offerings, a new one drawn when one is met. Each goal once a playthrough |
| **Acts as tiles** | One tap opens a sheet that says what the act does and costs, with the choices inside it. Only open acts are shown; an unaffordable one says so in four words |
| **Staffing by tapping** | Tap an office to see every candidate and what each would do in that post (when their trait is known) |
| **The telephone, played back** | After a Revelation, the chain is played one person at a time; each changed word is marked (struck through, or inserted). Then the carving, then your head in your hands if it went badly |
| **Villages inside regions, and a map** | A region now holds one to three villages. Souls belong to a village. The World tab draws the regions and villages as a pixel map: whose each village mostly is, where your missionary is working, and where the other gods live. Tap a village to see it and send your missionary. Moving your missionary to another region costs a season on the road; within a region it is free |
| **Shorter season report** | Changes as a row of figures, the news as a short list, then Pim |

## Not changed

The rules, the numbers of the measured game except where these changes force
a re-measure, the look (style tile 2), the writing rules.

## Measured (docs/BALANCE.md round 3)

Chapter 1 careful play 44% ±2.5 with the guided start (41% before; within
noise). 5.2 goals met a game, the first by season 3. Travel: Chapter 2 58%
against 22% staying home, Chapter 3 66% against 1%; thresholds rescaled, now
36% and 46%. Silence best in 8.2% of positions.

## What was to be measured

1. Careful play under the guided start, against the 30-40% target.
2. The goals: how many a careful player meets, and whether their rewards move
   the win rate (they should help, a little).
3. Travel: whether sending the missionary across regions is ever worth it.
4. The larger shapes again, now with villages.
